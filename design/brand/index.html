Buy like a chain. Stay independent.

Shoal lets independent shops buy together. The more shops join a buying round, the lower everyone's price. Money is reserved with PayPal, never taken until the round succeeds, and only the group price is charged.

Waiting for the first shop

Small fish, moving as one

One small fish is easy to push around. A shoal moves like something much bigger. Independent shops pay more than chains because they buy in small quantities; together, in a buying round, they buy like a chain. Our whole identity is that moment: many small dots gathering until, together, they form one big fish.

Coral, a shop. Each independent shop is one of the small dots. Warm, local, one of many.

Plum, the supplier. The producer who sets the price tiers and ships to every shop.

Berry, the round. The finished fish runs coral to berry to plum: every shop and the supplier in one shape. It's the only place our gradient appears.

Who we're talking to

Shops, our members
Cafés, bakeries, bookshops and boutiques that buy the same supplies as chains, in smaller quantities. They're busy and price-sensitive. Speak to them about what they save and that their money is safe until the round succeeds.
Suppliers, our partners
Small and mid-size producers selling direct. They set their own price tiers and minimums. Speak to them about committed demand, no chasing payments and one order instead of twenty.

Voice

We sound like the friendliest person at the local business association: practical, generous with the numbers, never salesy. Collective, not competitive.

Together
"We", "your round", "eight shops are in". The group is the hero.
Straight
Exact prices, exact savings, exact dates. No "up to" claims.
Reassuring
Always say where the money is: reserved, charged, or released.
Quietly clever
The AI does the coordination; we show the outcome, not the jargon.

How we talk to shops

Do
  • "Eight shops are in. Your price just dropped to $1.92 a carton."
  • "We've reserved $259.20 on PayPal. You'll pay the group price, never more."
  • "Round closed at $1.85. You paid $222.00; the other $37.20 was never taken."
Don't
  • "Hurry! Only 2 hours left to save BIG!" (fake urgency)
  • "Your card has been charged." (when it was only reserved)
  • "Save up to 40%!" (a number we can't promise)

How we talk to suppliers

Do
  • "1,440 cartons are committed and reserved, across 8 shops."
  • "You set the tiers and the minimum per delivery. We never go below them."
  • "90% is paid when you ship; the rest after deliveries are confirmed."
Don't
  • "Lower your prices to win our members." (pressure)
  • "Guaranteed orders every month." (we can't promise that)
  • Claims about volumes or results we haven't measured.

Logo: the Swimmy

Sixty-one small dots that, together, make one fish. Each dot is a shop; the group is the strength. The eye is the agent, outlined in ink so it reads on any background. On Jam and ink the dots switch to a lighter ramp (light coral, pink, lilac) that clears 4:1, so the tail never sinks into the ground. Below 48px, use the solid fish so the eye stays visible. In the product the fish is alive: it assembles dot by dot as shops join a round, glances at each new arrival, and smiles when the round closes.

Colour: Berry

A berry palette with one rule above all: the gradient belongs to the moment a round closes. Everything else is calm blush, deep Jam and clear ink, so the moment the fish completes is the most colourful thing on screen.

Five rules that keep it ours

  1. One gradient, one momentCoral → berry → plum appears only across the logo's dots and the completed fish. Never on backgrounds, buttons, cards or text.
  2. Jam, not blackDeep surfaces like this one, headline numbers and primary buttons use Jam, never near-black or navy.
  3. Plum, not electric violetThe supplier is a red-leaning purple that reads as fruit. Generic AI violets are off the table.
  4. Leaf for money savedLeaf green marks savings and settled payments, like $66.00 saved. Never decoration.
  5. Flat, never glowingDots are flat colour; role swatches get a soft glaze at most. No neon halos, glassmorphism or colour blurs.

Proportion

Blush 58%White 16%Ink 12%Jam 8%

Coral, berry and plum together stay under about 6% of a screen. Their rarity is what makes them land.

PayPal

PayPal's button is gold with navy text and handles every reservation and payment, so we never use gold, yellow or PayPal blue in our own UI. That keeps the PayPal button unmistakable and the two brands clearly separate. Always render PayPal's own buttons through their SDK; never restyle them.

Components

The building blocks for Shoal's surfaces: the shop's round, the supplier's view, the allocation and delivery views, and the agent's negotiations. AG Grid and Bryntum power the data-heavy views in the app; the previews here show the Shoal theme applied.

Round pool

The signature component. The fish starts as faint empty dots. Each shop that joins fills its share of the dots, in proportion to what it committed, and the tier ladder steps down as units cross each threshold. The agent's eye glances at every arrival. When the round closes the fish is complete: it gives one pulse, one ripple, and the eye smiles. Numbers sit outside the pool, so text never overlaps the fish.

Buttons, fields and status

ReservedRound openCapturedReleasedDeliveredClaim openResolved

Primary actions are Jam. Berry is reserved for joining a round and approving money moves. Status always pairs colour with a word, never colour alone. "Released" is the shop-facing word for a voided reservation.

Motion

Motion tells the story of the round: shops arrive, the price steps down, the group gathers and becomes one order. One authored moment per screen, never decoration for its own sake.

Gathering
Things move toward the group. Joining, stepping down a tier and closing all pull inward.
Soft, like sugar
Springy, slightly elastic easing. Dots fly in and settle into place; nothing snaps hard except a settled payment.
Settled is still
A closed round gets one clear moment (the fish completes, pulses once, a ripple, the eye smiles), then stillness. Calm signals it's done.

The living logo

The Swimmy's eye is the agent's presence, in the product and the brand. Try its states.

Idle blinks every 4–6 s and swims gently in place on the round page. As shops join it fills in dot by dot and glances at each arrival; it tilts while negotiating with the supplier; on a closed round it pulses once and the eye closes into a smile.

Accessibility

Fair means fair for everyone. These are requirements, not nice-to-haves.

Contrast
Body text at least 4.5:1 and dots and UI shapes at least 3:1 (WCAG 2.2 AA). Coral never carries white text; berry and Jam do.
Never colour alone
Shops and suppliers are always named; status chips pair colour with a word; tiers show the number of units.
Announce what changes
Tier changes, round outcomes and money moves are announced once in a polite live region: "Price dropped to $1.92 a carton."
Keyboard first
Quantity stepper, join, PayPal, claim and approval actions are reachable in order, with visible berry focus rings.
Reduced motion
All movement has a still equivalent; nothing essential depends on animation.
Plain language about money
Always say whether money is reserved, charged or released, and how much: "We've reserved $259.20. Nothing is charged until the round closes."

Research behind the choices

What shaped these guidelines. Most sources are secondary write-ups or vendor material, so treat them as direction, not proof.

  • Buying groups pool independent retailers' demand, often 50–300 members, with vendor-claimed savings of 10–25% (unverified). GPO guide; Amazon Business on GPOs
  • Group-buying platforms: deals lapse with no charge if the threshold isn't reached. Group buying platforms
  • Split shipping to many member locations is a standard buying-group expectation. Buying groups and split shipping
  • Adjacent wholesale channels: fixed-price marketplaces and native B2B price lists. Faire overview; Shopify B2B for all plans
  • PayPal's direction: a commerce platform, AI-powered commerce, small business. PYMNTS on PayPal 2.0
  • Colour fit matters more than universal meanings; saturation reads as excitement, depth as competence. Labrecque & Milne, JAMS 2012
  • Purple or indigo in only 2 of 42 finance design systems sampled (directional). Lazyweb, 2026
  • Red prices lower perceived value on modest discounts, so prices are never red. Frontiers in Psychology, 2022
  • PayPal button colours and styling options. PayPal buttons style guide
  • Agentic UX: explicit confirmation for money moves and a clear record of what happened. Smashing Magazine, 2026
